Getting there

  • The room is on the ground floor, and it is step-free from the pavement to the chair you sit in.
  • There is an accessible toilet on the same floor.
  • There is parking on Fromefield, two minutes’ walk away, and it is free after six.
  • It is eight minutes on foot from the market place, uphill on the way here. The hill is lit the whole way up.
  • The buses along Bath Road stop at the top of Fromefield. The last one back into town is at 22:40.

What the course is

You arrive at seven. The chairs are already in a circle, the lamps are on, and there are seven other people who booked the same four Wednesdays. We go round once, first names only, and you can say as little as you like.

Each evening runs two hours and has one thing in it. The first is attention — how to tell the difference between what you are sensing and what you are supplying. The second and third are hands: your own first, then the person sitting opposite you. The fourth has no plan, and by then it does not need one.

A place is a place on all four evenings. It is one booking, one price, and one chair that stays yours whether you come or not — you are booking the run, not the seventh of October. If you can only make two of the four, this is the wrong thing to book, and a one-day workshop is probably the right one.

You stay clothed and seated throughout, and nobody touches you. You can stop at any point, and you do not have to say why. No belief is asked of you. People arrive sceptical and leave sceptical, and four Wednesdays is a perfectly good way to spend October.

The four evenings

In order, each two hours, in the same room with the same eight people. Open one to read what happens in it.

  • Evening 1 Sitting still, and what that actually is Wed 7 Oct · 19:00–21:00 · The Garden Room

    Introductions, then two hours on attention. You work on your own hands first, at arm’s length, and we go round afterwards and say what that was like. Most people find they have been doing this for years without a name for it.

  • Evening 2 Working in pairs Wed 14 Oct · 19:00–21:00 · The Garden Room

    You sit opposite someone from the circle, an arm’s length apart, and take both turns — twenty minutes giving, a swap, twenty minutes receiving. Receiving is the harder half, and almost nobody expects that.

  • Evening 3 The longer sequence Wed 21 Oct · 19:00–21:00 · The Garden Room

    One sequence worked slowly from the head down to the feet, once through without stopping, while the rest of the circle watches. Everybody is watched once and watches three times, and we say what we saw afterwards rather than during.

  • Evening 4 Whatever the room is carrying Wed 28 Oct · 19:00–21:00 · The Garden Room

    No plan. We work through whatever has come up since the seventh, out loud and in the circle, and finish at nine. There is tea afterwards for anyone who wants to stay, and I say plainly what there is to do next if you want to keep going.

What to bring

Almost nothing. There is nothing to buy between evenings and nothing you need to have read.

  • Tea and something to eat are on the table at eight each evening. Tell me what you cannot eat when you book.
  • Wear whatever you can sit comfortably in for two hours.
  • Bring a notebook if you are someone who writes things down. About half the room usually is, and on a course it tends to be more by the third week.
  • Bring a jumper. The room is warm at seven and cooler by nine, and it is October.

Before the first evening

When you book, a short note comes with your confirmation — two pages on what to expect, and one question to sit with before the seventh. You do not have to do anything with it, and nobody asks about it on the night.
