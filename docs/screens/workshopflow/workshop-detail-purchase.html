What the day is

You arrive at ten. There is coffee, and a circle of chairs, and nine other people who also did not quite know what to expect.

The morning is spent on attention — how to tell the difference between what you are sensing and what you are supplying. Most people find they have been doing this for years without a name for it.

After lunch you work in pairs. You stay clothed, seated, and at arm's length throughout. Nothing is asked of your beliefs, and you can stop at any point without explaining why.

We finish at half past four, and there is tea for anyone who wants to stay and talk.

Practicalities
