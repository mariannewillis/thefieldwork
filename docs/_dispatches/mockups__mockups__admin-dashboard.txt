### [supersede-generic] night-side-emanation
# Night-Side Emanation — bespoke identity for The Field Work

## Intent

The supplied mark is a radiating field — concentric light running outward from a
centre (brief §2). This skill makes that geometry the design's subject: light is
the thing, not decoration laid over it. The register is night-side — low light,
stillness, an adult in a quiet room, "rather than like a spa brochure"
(brief §2). It departs from a measured category norm: 5 of 6 researched sites
are cream/near-white ground with exactly one warm earth or metallic accent, and
**not one competitor uses magenta or a gold→magenta gradient**
(competitors.md). The differentiation move is a
gold→magenta radial emanation and stillness, instead of an acid flat accent and
Webflow motion (styles.md Style 1).

## When it applies

- palette · page-ground · background · hero · imagery — wherever a ground
  colour, a gradient, or one of the commissioned plates is placed.
- BOTH light-ground and dark-ground directions. Night-side governs the FEELING
  (still, low-lit, unhurried), not the literal value: a light-ground direction
  is not disqualified, it just cannot be spa-bright.
- It does not decide composition; which colour becomes the page ground stays a
  per-direction decision.

## Do

- **Use the gradient as EMANATION** — light running outward from a centre or an
  edge, with real falloff. One emanation per screen, placed where the eye should
  rest; two competing centres cancel each other.
- **Treat the palette as POSITION-DEPENDENT and verify before use.** Computed
  figures (WCAG 2.2 relative luminance) from `assets/brand/README.md` — read it
  rather than trusting a remembered number:
  - `gold #E9C87E` is a **text** colour on the dark plums (10.89:1 on plum-800
    `#2B0E28`) and a **non-text** colour on light (1.47:1 on blush, 1.55:1 on
    ivory — failing AA body *and* the 3:1 large-text *and* non-text floors). On a
    light ground gold may be a decorative mark, edge or fill; never type, never a
    functional border, never a focus ring.
  - `magenta-deep #C2187A` **inverts**: text-safe on light (5.18:1 on blush),
    fill-only on dark (3.10:1 on plum-800; 2.82:1 on plum-700, where it misses
    even the 3:1 non-text floor).
  - `magenta #D6338A` fails AA body on **both** ground families (4.08:1 on
    blush, 3.93:1 on plum-800). It is not body type anywhere in this system.
  - `rose #E85D9B` is the dark-ground text-safe pink (5.41:1 on plum-800), but
    drops to 4.92:1 on plum-700 — so it clears AA body on the darker two only.
- **Hold non-text to 3:1 against its ADJACENT colour** (WCAG 2.2 SC 1.4.11):
  borders, rules, focus rings, input outlines, icon strokes — measured against
  the ground they actually sit on, not against the page. A hairline in any
  mid-gradient stop will usually miss it. This is the check that gets skipped
  because it is invisible by eye.
- **Never set a mid-gradient stop as body text without computing it first**, and
  state the figure alongside it. If a light direction wants gold *type*, derive
  a value (`#8A6A22` = 4.61:1 on blush) rather than reusing the artwork's gold.
- **Let the photography carry the warmth.** The commissioned plates are dark,
  warm, high-contrast, with gold-to-magenta light in them; a light-ground
  direction must state how it handles them or it ships a register collision.

## Don't

- **No lavender, mandalas, crystals, chakra wheels, soft-focus serenity, or
  cupped-hands-around-a-glowing-orb.** The brief names the soft-focus lavender
  register as the thing this brand must defeat; `aura-hands-between` is the
  single most-reproduced composition in the category and is reserve, never hero.
- **Don't use the gradient as a button fill, a diagonal banner, or a rainbow
  band.** Those are decoration; emanation has a centre and a falloff.
- **Don't read night-side as "make everything dark".** Still and low-lit is the
  requirement. Spa-bright is the failure.
- **Don't carry a colour's role across grounds.** This supersedes the generic
  taste-default "apply the brand colours consistently" — consistency here means
  the same colours doing *different jobs* on different grounds. This exact
  failure has shipped on this project before; it is invisible to anyone who
  already knows what the text says, so it must be computed, not reviewed.
- **Don't reach for the category palette** — a cream ground with one warm earth
  or metallic accent (measured: `#E1CCBE`, `#AB8041`, `#BFAD99`, `#EFE3B8`).
  Where the direction is dark, differentiate from the one dark exemplar
  (Othership `#372338`, a US bathhouse) on radial emanation and stillness — not

### [supersede-generic] plain-declarative-voice
# Plain declarative voice — bespoke identity for The Field Work

## Intent

Write what happens, not what it will do for them. This is **simultaneously a
craft rule and this project's legal position**, and it must be treated as both.
The UK CAP Code (enforced by the ASA) restricts health-outcome claims for
therapies without robust evidence; energy healing has no accepted clinical
evidence base, so the site describes what happens, what people report, and what
the practitioner does — and does not promise outcomes (brief §14).

That constraint is not a tax, it is the opening. Category research found the copy
default is either clinical-explanatory or breathless-transformational with
nothing in between; 3 of 6 practitioner sites make a claim needing
substantiation, 1 of 6 carries a disclaimer. Plain declarative speech is
therefore the unoccupied register **and** the compliant one — and the sceptical,
curious adult this brand speaks to can feel the difference (brief §2, §1.3).

## When it applies

Every word: headlines, body, CTA labels, form helper text, empty states, alt
text, and admin copy the owner will imitate. Highest stakes on `home` beat 5 —
"what this is not", the compliance surface and the trust surface at once (brief
§11, §14) — plus `about` and every service description. Placeholder copy
included: **no lorem, ever.** The proposition is that a real person is speaking.

## Do

- **Work the PERMITTED zone deliberately — don't hedge to nothing.** ASA/CAP
  "Health: Reiki" expressly permits claims about the emotional and spiritual
  effects of the therapy, its relaxing nature, its meditative qualities, an
  improvement in a feeling of overall wellbeing and an improved sense of self,
  and about the practitioner's professionalism and the therapy surroundings.
  "Health: Healing therapy" adds spiritual and emotional comfort and support
  during illness, grief and stress. A real, usable vocabulary — use it.
- **Describe procedure, room and hour.** What happens, in what order, how long
  it lasts, what you wear, whether anyone touches you. Concrete nouns, short
  sentences, no adjective stacks.
- **Lead with the refusal where a refusal is honest.** Mettle Health opens with
  what it cannot do — _"We can't change your situation, but we can help you live
  with it."_ Take the move: state the limit first, then the offer. It buys more
  trust than another testimonial would.
- **Report, don't promise.** Attribute experience to the person who had it
  ("people describe…"). "What it will do for you" is a claim; "what happens, and
  what people report" is description.
- **Spend the specificity budget on the unasked question.** _Illustrative only:_
  "You keep your clothes on. Nobody touches you." beats "a safe, welcoming
  space" — and brief §15 makes it measurable: ≥80% of test visitors should be
  able to state that fact unprompted after one scroll.
- **Treat credentials as UNWRITTEN until confirmed.** The practitioner's name
  and her years practising and teaching are explicitly unverified (brief §20).
  Never assert them in copy. Use a visibly marked placeholder and flag it for
  client confirmation before that surface ships.

## Don't

- **Breathless transformation** — "heal your life", "release your blocks",
  "step into your power". The register the brief bans by name (§2: never
  breathless).
- **The clinical-explanatory modality lecture** — _"Reiki is a Japanese
  technique for…"_. Measured as a category default in the competitor set; also
  banned by §2 (never clinical).
- **The outcome-adjective list** — "Confident, Happy, Calm, Relaxed, Loved,
  Safe." A promise wearing a word cloud.
- **Any named medical condition, and any treat / cure / diagnose / prevent /
  alleviate verb** — physical or psychological. Two upheld ASA rulings are on
  point: Allan Sweeney Reiki (20 July 2011 — ADHD, back pain, depression,
  cancer) and Healing on the Streets, Bath (13 June 2013).
- **Anything that could discourage essential medical treatment.** CAP is
  explicit; both upheld rulings turned on it. A visible "complementary, not a
  substitute for medical care" statement is required (brief §14).
- **The soft-focus lavender wellness register** — and equally the generic "warm,
  friendly, benefit-led" default of the shipped taste skills.
  `precedence: supersede-generic`: where a generic heuristic asks for a benefit
  headline or an emotional promise, **this skill wins.**

# Dated things are typeset — bespoke technique for The Fieldwork

## Intent

A schedule is content, not chrome. The category hides it: 3 of 6 measured
practitioner/studio sites publish no price at all, and Re:Mind's `/book-class`
schedule returned "No results found" to a static fetch because Momence renders it
client-side (competitors.md). This project's trust position is the opposite — the
Carrier persona names "a price that only appears after you make contact" as the
thing that breaks their day (brief.md §6), and the products block renders live
Courses, Workshops and Services at equal billing with real dates and prices
(brief.md §12). So the listing is typeset as the primary content, on the
repertory-cinema model: the DATE is the section header, each time beneath it a
standalone anchor. The mechanics below are transferable to any dated, priced,
bookable thing; the necessity is this client's.

## When it applies

- Products block · the three index pages · offering detail · the course session
  list · the service free-slot picker.
- Anywhere a card would otherwise carry a date, a duration, a capacity or a fee.
- Not on prose surfaces (about, privacy) — they have no rows to align.

## Do

1. **Date as a section header at display scale; each offering under it a
   standalone line.** Group chronologically, set the date on the display ramp
   ("Saturday 25 July"), and make each time/offering beneath it a discrete
   tappable target — a rule-separated row, not a rectangle with a shadow. This
   solves scan-by-time and scan-by-event in one layout (inspirations.md Insp. 22,
   Metrograph).
2. **State the price. Always, on the listing, not only on detail.** In the same
   typographic voice as everything else — a fee is a fact, not a badge. No "from",
   no "enquire", no price behind a form (competitors.md §"Publish the price on the
   page").
3. **Tabular lining figures, set explicitly** — `font-variant-numeric:
tabular-nums lining-nums` on every date, time, duration, capacity and price, and
   align columns of figures on their numerals. Choose a body face that actually
   ships them (inspirations.md §type pairings). A column of times that doesn't
   align cannot be scanned, which defeats the whole move.
4. **Equal billing across offering kinds.** One register, one price list, one
   surface. Distinguish kinds by their DATA — a span with n sessions inside it, a
   single dated point, a bookable duration — never by giving one kind a richer
   card (brief.md §12; Insp. 26 run-span typesetting).
5. **Design the empty state as a first-class target.** Organise the surface around
   "what is on now" so it degrades gracefully to one item, or none: a plain
   sentence naming what is coming and one route to ask, at the same type scale as
   a populated row. A grid with one card in it looks broken; a programme with one
   entry does not (brief.md §11 "zero workshops on day one"; Insp. 29).
6. **Render every date, time and price server-side, in the initial HTML.** A
   schedule that only exists after JavaScript does not exist for a crawler, a slow
   connection, or assistive tech. A client fetch may refresh a slot's live
   availability; it must never be what puts the date on the page (competitors.md
   §"Server-render the schedule").

## Don't

- **Don't push dates behind filters or a separate calendar view.** Replace the
  filter-first affordance with an already-sorted chronological list carrying the
  date on every row — Barbican demotes chronology to theme filters and the visitor
  who came for a date can't find one (Insp. 25).
- **Don't wrap listings in shadowed, rounded, image-topped cards.** Replace the
  card with a typographic row: date header, title, one small metadata line
  (duration · format · price), a rule. The container is the type, not a box.
- **Don't write "contact for pricing", "POA", or "places" without a figure.**
  Replace with the actual number; where capacity genuinely can't be promised, use
  an honest inline phrase ("Places available" / "Limited places") stated as
  information, never a live seat count (brief.md §5; Insp. 23).
- **Don't let a third-party booking widget typeset the schedule.** It arrives with
  its own type, figures, radii and motion and breaks the surface visibly

  non-animated state change on selection; a held slot's countdown is text, not a
  spinner (inspirations.md §motion — Slot selection).

### [augment-generic] stillness-as-motion-budget
# Stillness as motion budget — bespoke technique for The Fieldwork

## Intent

Treat motion as a fixed budget with exactly one line item. The whole allowance
goes to a single slow move that MEANS something; everything else on the page
holds still. The researched category spends its motion on scroll fade-ins and a
testimonial carousel and gets nothing back — no site in the set uses motion as
meaning. The one motion-rich exemplar is loud in a way that would break a brand
whose stated register is "unhurried, quietly certain." So the differentiating
move is not more motion or better motion: it is arithmetic. One move, slow
enough to read as atmosphere rather than as effect, on a page that is otherwise
still.

## When it applies

- Any motion, transition, hero treatment, or ambient/background effect, on any surface.
- Marketing and application surfaces alike. On dense-data, settings, or form
  surfaces the budget is zero, not one.

## Do

- **Pick ONE move and spend the whole budget on it.** It expresses the concept
  rather than decorating it. Ambient drift is measured in seconds, not
  milliseconds — a ~12-second cycle the eye barely registers is the target, not
  a 400ms ease.
- **Go slower than feels right.** If a movement reads as an effect, it is
  already too fast. Halve the speed and re-judge.
- **Keep the one move decorative-only.** It never carries content, never encodes
  state, and is never the only way to learn something.
- **Author every motion `prefers-reduced-motion`-first, and check the still
  frame is a FINISHED composition** — not the animated one with a hole in it. If
  disabling the animation leaves an empty region, a blank panel, or a missing
  focal element, the motion was load-bearing and the design is wrong. Fix the
  composition, don't re-enable the motion.
- **Functional micro-transitions are exempt from the budget but subject to the
  ceiling** — focus rings, hover state change, open/close of a disclosure. Under
  ~200ms, never elaborated into a flourish.

## Don't — the static-render prohibition (HARD)

**NEVER gate content behind `opacity: 0` plus an IntersectionObserver, a
scroll-reveal library, or any JS-driven entrance.** Not on any element, not
"just the hero", not with a fallback timer. This is a prohibition, not a
preference.

**The page must render WHOLE in a static screenshot, with no JavaScript executed
and no scrolling.** Every element sits at final opacity and final position in
the delivered HTML/CSS. Motion is a progressive enhancement applied to
already-visible content — never a condition of seeing it.

Two reasons, both hard. It is the accessibility floor: content that requires JS
plus a scroll to appear is content some people never get. And it is a
mockup-safety rule: `lint-mockup-safe.mjs` enforces it mechanically, and a
violation makes the page render blank or half-empty to a static judge. **This
exact regression has shipped on this project's predecessors** — sections present
in the markup, invisible in review.

Also don't:

- **No scroll-triggered fade-ins, reveals, or staggered entrances.** The category
  default, and the thing the prohibition above exists to kill.
- **No carousels, parallax stacks, marquees, or hover flourishes.** Each is a
  second thing moving; with two moving things neither is the signature.
- **Don't let motion substitute for composition.** If the layout only works once
  things have animated in, the layout is the bug.

## Precedence

`augment-generic` — layers a hard budget ceiling and the static-render
prohibition on top of generic frontend / design-taste motion guidance. Where
generic taste suggests scroll reveals or entrance animation, this skill wins.

## Keep the-lit-clearing's IDENTITY — this is not a re-skin of the reference

The visual world stays exactly as the approved landing page:

- Ground: plum `#160712`, raised surface `#260F20`.
- Clearings: blush `#FBF3F1` panels carrying plum ink. On the admin surface these
  become PANELS — the working surface of the portal — not one hero pool.
- Display type: Cormorant Garamond, used sparingly and at a working size (a
  single page title, the big numbers in the triage rows). Body: Source Sans 3 at
  17-18px. Machine references and tabular figures: Azeret Mono.
- Accent rules are CONTEXT-LOCKED and this is load-bearing for legibility:
  magenta `#C2187A` is POOL-ONLY (3.45:1 on the dark plate fails; 5.18:1 inside a
  blush panel passes). Gold `#E9C87E` is PLATE-ONLY (10.89:1 on dark; 1.47:1 in a
  panel fails). Use gold for labels on the dark chrome, magenta for emphasis and
  actions inside panels. Never swap them.
- Hard edges throughout — the direction excludes border-radius, box-shadow and
  cards. Rows are separated by hairline rules, not by cards. The one sanctioned
  curve on the whole design is a true circle for an avatar.
- Logo: `assets/brand/logo-horizontal.svg` in the sidebar head, referenced as an
  `<img>`. Never redrawn.

## What the application surface CHANGES about the concept

The landing page's governing idea — one clearing of light per viewport, opening
as you scroll — is a MARKETING device. On a portal it is a category error, and
the direction's own `signatureMoveScope` decides how far it travels. Here:

- There is no one-clearing-per-viewport rule. A dashboard legitimately shows
  several panels at once.
- There is no growth ladder. Panels are sized by what they hold.
- There is no full-bleed photography behind the working surface. The dark ground
  may carry a very low-luminance plate for atmosphere at the page edges, but
  never behind data.
- The concept survives as the EDGE: hard-cut blush panels on a dark plum ground,
  nothing crossing a panel boundary, the same ink discipline. That is the family
  resemblance to the landing page — not the hero device.

## The interface-craft levers this screen must satisfy

- **State-completeness** — the triage list needs its empty state designed too
  ("nothing needs you tonight"), not just the populated one.
- **One primary action per row, and one for the screen** — the eye must find the
  thing to do in under two seconds.
- **Scan hierarchy** — the single most important datum (how many things need her
  tonight, and the oldest one) readable at a glance before any prose.
- **Density done right** — this is a working surface; it should feel closer to a
  ledger than to a brochure. Data-ink over chrome.
- **Affordance legibility** — actions look like actions; the destructive ones are
  guarded.
- **Progressive disclosure** — the detail lives behind the row, not dumped on it.
